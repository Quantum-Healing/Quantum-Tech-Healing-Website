Scalar Waves & Quantum Biology

The physics

Scalar waves exist in the longitudinal plane — outside of the conventional transverse electromagnetic spectrum.

Classical electromagnetic theory describes waves that oscillate perpendicular to their direction of travel (transverse waves). Scalar electromagnetic waves, by contrast, oscillate in the same direction as travel — making them longitudinal in nature, akin to sound waves but in the electromagnetic domain. They were first mathematically described by James Clerk Maxwell in his original 1865 quaternion equations, before being subsequently omitted from the later simplified versions of his equations now taught in mainstream physics.

Nikola Tesla later demonstrated their physical existence experimentally, and modern researchers including Tom Bearden and Konstantin Meyl have developed more complete theoretical frameworks for understanding scalar fields and their interaction with biological systems.

Quantum biology

Living cells are quantum systems — and respond to quantum-level interventions.

Quantum biology is a rapidly growing scientific discipline that applies quantum mechanics to biological systems. Research has demonstrated quantum effects in:

  • Photosynthesis: Plants use quantum coherence to transfer energy with near-perfect efficiency.
  • Enzyme function: Quantum tunnelling enables enzymes to catalyse reactions that would otherwise be chemically impossible.
  • DNA mutation: Quantum effects may play a role in spontaneous genetic mutations.
  • Consciousness: The Penrose-Hameroff “Orchestrated Objective Reduction” hypothesis proposes that quantum processes in microtubules within neurons may be foundational to conscious experience.

The RASHA operates by generating scalar fields that interact with the body at this quantum-biological level — supporting the coherence and resonance of cellular systems.

The History of Scalar Healing Technology
1880s

Nikola Tesla — The Discovery of Scalar Waves

Serbian-American inventor Nikola Tesla first described and demonstrated what he called “non-Hertzian” waves — energy that could be transmitted through space without conventional electromagnetic propagation. Tesla believed this energy could be used to transmit power wirelessly across the globe, and conducted many experiments exploring its properties. His Wardenclyffe Tower project was designed to harness this very energy. While many of his ideas were suppressed or dismissed in his lifetime, modern quantum physics has confirmed the existence of scalar-type fields, vindicating much of his pioneering work.
